Insider Threat
Why Insider Threats Are Still the Most Underestimated Risk in Corporate Security

Every organization worries about the hacker in a hoodie sitting in a basement somewhere, running scripts against their network. It's the image that dominates boardroom conversations, cybersecurity budgets, and news headlines. But while companies pour resources into firewalls, endpoint detection, and threat intelligence platforms aimed at external actors, the most damaging breaches often come from someone who already has a badge, a login, and a reason to be there.

Insider threats remain one of the most underestimated risks in corporate security — not because organizations don't know they exist, but because they're uncomfortable to confront, difficult to detect, and easy to rationalize away until it's too late.

The Numbers Don't Lie

The data on insider threats is sobering. According to the Ponemon Institute, the average cost of an insider threat incident exceeds $15 million annually when factoring in detection, containment, investigation, and remediation. More telling is the trend — insider threat incidents have increased significantly over the past several years, and the time to contain them averages over 85 days.

That's nearly three months of an active threat operating inside your environment before it's stopped.

Why Detection Is So Hard

External threats trigger alerts. A port scan lights up a SIEM. Malware behavior matches known signatures. Anomalies get flagged. But an insider threat often looks completely normal — because it is normal, until it isn't. An employee accessing the HR system at 11 PM might be finishing a legitimate project. Or they might be harvesting employee data before they resign. The behavior is identical. The intent is invisible.

The Physical and Digital Divide Creates Blind Spots

One of the most overlooked aspects of insider threat risk is the gap between physical security and cybersecurity programs. In most organizations, these two disciplines operate independently. The team managing access control badges rarely talks to the team managing network access logs. This siloed approach creates dangerous blind spots that insider threats exploit because they know the organization from the inside.

What a Strong Insider Threat Program Looks Like

Least privilege access ensures employees can only access what they need. Behavioral baselines establish what normal looks like, making anomalies detectable. Cross-functional intelligence sharing connects physical security observations, HR flags, and cyber monitoring into a unified picture. A reporting culture encourages employees to surface concerns early, before a warning sign becomes a full incident.

The most dangerous threat to your organization might already be inside the building — or already logged into your network. The question isn't whether you'll face an insider threat. The question is whether you'll be ready when you do.

The Hidden Cost of Doing Nothing: Why Security Companies That Don't Publish Lose Clients

There's a conversation happening right now between your prospect and your competitor. It isn't happening in a sales meeting or over the phone. It's happening on a screen, at 10 PM, while your prospect researches their options before making a decision that could be worth tens of thousands of dollars to whoever wins the contract.

Your competitor published an article last month on ransomware response planning. They published another on physical security integration. Their blog has fourteen posts that speak directly to the problems your prospect is trying to solve. And when that prospect searched for answers, your competitor's name kept showing up. Yours didn't.

Content Is How Trust Gets Built Before You Ever Meet

The security industry runs on trust. Clients aren't just buying a product or a service — they're trusting you with their most sensitive assets, their risk exposure, their people, and in many cases their reputation. That level of trust doesn't get handed over because of a slick pitch deck. It gets built over time, through repeated exposure to your thinking, your expertise, and your perspective on the problems they care about most.

The Search Reality No One Talks About

Here's a fact worth sitting with: 67% of B2B buyers have already done significant research before they ever contact a vendor. By the time someone fills out your contact form, they've likely already formed an opinion about whether you know what you're talking about. That opinion was shaped by what they found — or didn't find — when they searched.

What Consistent Publishing Actually Does for Your Brand

A single article doesn't transform your pipeline. But twelve articles over the course of a year — each one targeted, well-written, and genuinely useful — builds something that's very difficult for competitors to replicate quickly. It improves your search visibility. It gives your sales team something to share. It gives existing clients confidence that they made the right choice.

The Question Worth Asking

If a prospect searched your company's name right now and spent ten minutes on your website, would they walk away convinced that your team is the most knowledgeable option in the market? Would they find recent, relevant content that speaks directly to the challenges keeping them up at night?

If the honest answer is anything less than yes, the cost of doing nothing is already adding up. The companies winning on content right now aren't necessarily the ones with the biggest budgets. They're the ones that made a decision to show up consistently.
